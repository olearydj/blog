# Astra X thread

Published September 5, 2026: [12-post thread](https://x.com/Antisimplistic/status/2096349863554302130). The API verified the full reply chain. Posts 2–9 include the existing article screenshots, including the gameplay frame for Gogh Strike. Original source links remain in the text.

1/12 My timeline has been overrun by Astra, OpenAI's GPT-6. With Codex, I collected 82 demos and evaluations.

These look like a big jump in capability. Reliability is still an open question. A few that caught my eye, with more here:
https://antisimplistic.com/posts/2026-09-05-astra-first-48-hours/

---

2/12 Dan Shipper's Waterloo was one of the first. You can stand behind either formation, pause at the volley, and inspect the encounter.

As a teacher, I can see uses for this immediately. The source notes distinguish history from interpretation.
https://x.com/danshipper/status/2095880567313092770

---

3/12 Matt Shumer built a drivable Manhattan in Unreal. There's something about seeing a familiar city at street level that makes the scale register.

His account describes roughly a week of work, existing assets, and substantial compute.
https://x.com/mattshumer_/status/2095609734845927525

---

4/12 This Onshape robot is nuts. @Alpha10six describes changes to a camera bracket, port access, and ventilation while Codex edits the assembly.

Those are the fiddly details that make a design usable. He controls the view; the agent handles the edits.
https://x.com/Alpha10six/status/2096261751389978640

---

5/12 Gogh Strike puts painters in a shooter, but the painted streets are what got me. The rendering carries the idea through the world you move around in.

Peter Gostev published the code, too. I'd like to see more games take this kind of visual risk.
https://x.com/petergostev/status/2096013280519016608

---

6/12 @ashebytes made a male anatomy model with an exploded view and 2,234 pieces, reportedly. You can pull the body apart to inspect how things fit together.

I'd want an anatomist to check it before teaching from it. The teaching possibilities are clear.
https://x.com/ashebytes/status/2096221988763173186

---

7/12 Mehran brought a fly simulation into the browser, with neural and sensory readouts alongside the moving animal.

Astra helped integrate existing scientific work into something easier to inspect and share. Biological accuracy is a separate question.
https://x.com/mehrantsi/status/2096282317148885453

---

8/12 Pietro Schirano's Ableton demo leaves you with instruments, notes, and an arrangement you can keep editing.

That matters to me. The next request could change a bass line or replace an instrument. Listen and judge the music for yourself.
https://x.com/skirano/status/2095595942544089525

---

9/12 Parallax Desk responds to your head position: lean sideways and nearby windows shift differently from distant ones, revealing what's behind them.

I haven't tested the head tracking, but it's an intriguing way to rethink overlapping windows.
https://x.com/outscape/status/2096286659566309605

---

10/12 Epoch's Erdős evaluation gives us something firmer to judge: Astra solved two of 68 open problems under the benchmark protocol, with proofs checked in Lean.

Additional attempts brought the total to five. Different budgets and setups matter here.
https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos

11/12 Ethan Mollick's living wiki is close to my interests: correspondence, calendars, and writing turned into a maintained resource with recurring briefings.

It's private, so we can't inspect the result. I'd want every summary traceable to its sources.
https://x.com/emollick/status/2095606622055760159
